Problem 1: Equilibrium and Price Supports

The table below shows the demand and supply schedules for soybeans.

Price Quantity Demanded Quantity Supplied
$7.20 10 40
$7.00 15 35
$6.80 20 30
$6.60 25 25
$6.40 30 20
$6.20 35 15

Quantities are in thousands of bushels.

  1. What is the equilibrium price of soybeans?
  2. How many thousand bushels will be exchanged at that price?
  3. If the federal government supports a price of $7.00 per bushel, is there a shortage or a surplus, and how many thousand bushels?
Show Answer
  1. Equilibrium price is $6.60, where quantity demanded equals quantity supplied.
  2. 25 thousand bushels are exchanged at equilibrium.
  3. At the $7.00 price support, quantity demanded is 15 and quantity supplied is 35. That is a surplus of 20 thousand bushels, the government’s price floor sits above equilibrium, so sellers offer more than buyers want at that price.

Problem 2: Equilibrium with a Price Ceiling

The table below summarizes the market for principles of economics textbooks.

Price Quantity Demanded Quantity Supplied
$40 4,200 200
$50 2,200 600
$60 1,200 1,200
$70 700 2,000
$80 550 3,000
  1. What is the market equilibrium price and quantity?
  2. A college places a $50 price ceiling on textbooks to control costs for students. How many textbooks actually get sold at that price?
  3. While the ceiling is in effect, a new printing technology lowers production costs, shifting supply to the right. What happens to the number of textbooks actually sold?
Show Answer
  1. Equilibrium is $60, where quantity demanded and quantity supplied both equal 1,200.
  2. At the $50 ceiling, quantity demanded is 2,200 but quantity supplied is only 600. The number of textbooks actually sold is capped by the shorter side of the market, so only 600 get sold, even though far more students want one at that price. This leaves a shortage of 1,600 books.
  3. A rightward shift in supply means more textbooks are supplied at every price, including $50. As long as the ceiling is still below the new equilibrium price, more textbooks get sold than before (still capped by supply, but a higher supply quantity), narrowing the shortage.
