Practice Problems

Warm-Up: Quick Concept and Calculation Checks

  1. In Year 1, Priya’s disposable income is $35,000 and she consumes $32,000. In Year 2, her disposable income rises to $40,000 and she consumes $35,600. What is her MPC out of that $5,000 raise?
  2. Using your answer to (a), what is Priya’s MPS out of that raise?
  3. For each situation, say whether it causes a movement along the consumption schedule or a shift of the whole schedule, and if a shift, which direction: (i) disposable income rises,
    1. a booming stock market makes households feel wealthier, (iii) banks make credit cards easier to get, (iv) real interest rates rise sharply, encouraging more saving.
  4. A firm is considering four possible investment projects with these expected rates of return: Project W, 10 percent; Project X, 8 percent; Project Y, 4 percent; Project Z, 2 percent. If the real interest rate is 5 percent, which projects should the firm undertake?
  5. True or False: investment spending tends to be more stable than consumption spending over the business cycle.
  6. If the MPS rises, does the simple spending multiplier (\(1/MPS\)) get larger or smaller?
Show Answer
  1. \(MPC = \Delta C / \Delta DI = (35{,}600 - 32{,}000)/(40{,}000-35{,}000) = 3{,}600/5{,}000 = 0.72\).
  2. \(MPS = 1 - MPC = 1 - 0.72 = 0.28\).
    1. Movement along the schedule. (ii) Shift up (the wealth effect increases consumption at every income level). (iii) Shift up (easier credit supports more consumption at every income level). (iv) Shift down in consumption, shift up in saving (higher real rates reward saving over spending at every income level).
  4. Undertake any project whose expected return exceeds the 5 percent real interest rate: Project W (10 percent) and Project X (8 percent). Projects Y and Z do not clear the 5 percent hurdle.
  5. False. Investment is considerably more volatile than consumption, since capital purchases can be postponed and profit expectations shift quickly.
  6. Smaller. A higher MPS means more of each new dollar of income leaks into saving rather than being re-spent, so each round of the spending chain is smaller and the total multiplied effect is smaller.

Problem 1: Investment Demand and the Real Interest Rate

A small city has five local businesses each considering one investment project:

Business Project Expected Rate of Return
Bakery New oven 11%
Garage Second work bay 8%
Cafe Outdoor seating 6%
Bookstore Storefront renovation 4%
Gym New equipment 2%
  1. If the real interest rate is 5 percent, which projects get undertaken?
  2. Suppose the real interest rate falls to 3 percent. Which additional project(s), if any, now become profitable?
  3. Explain, in your own words, why a falling real interest rate increases the total quantity of investment demanded in an economy.
  4. Name two factors, other than the interest rate, that could shift the entire investment demand curve to the right (more investment demanded at every interest rate).
Show Answer
  1. At 5 percent: the Bakery (11%), Garage (8%), and Cafe (6%) projects clear the hurdle.
  2. At 3 percent: the Bookstore (4%) project now also clears the hurdle, joining the other three.
  3. As the real interest rate falls, projects that were previously unprofitable (their expected return was below the cost of borrowing) become profitable, so more investment gets undertaken. This is exactly why the investment demand curve slopes downward.
  4. Any two of: improving technology that raises expected returns, falling costs of acquiring or operating capital, lower business taxes, or more optimistic business expectations about future demand.

Problem 2: The Multiplier in Action

A new employer moves to town and immediately spends $80 million building a facility. Suppose the MPC in this local economy is 0.6.

  1. What is the MPS?
  2. What is the simple spending multiplier?
  3. What is the total eventual change in local income from this initial $80 million in spending?
  4. Show the first four rounds of the spending chain (the initial spending, then each subsequent round of re-spending).
  5. If local residents instead had an MPC of only 0.4, would the total impact on local income be larger or smaller than in part (c)? Explain in one sentence.
Show Answer
  1. \(MPS = 1 - 0.6 = 0.4\).
  2. Multiplier \(= 1/MPS = 1/0.4 = 2.5\).
  3. Total change in income \(= \$80\text{M} \times 2.5 = \$200\) million.
  4. Round 1: \(80.00\) million (the initial spending). Round 2: \(80 \times 0.6 = \$48.00\) million. Round 3: \(48.00 \times 0.6 = \$28.80\) million. Round 4: \(28.80 \times 0.6 \approx \$17.28\) million. Each round is smaller than the last, since some of each round’s income leaks into saving rather than being re-spent.
  5. Smaller. A lower MPC of 0.4 means a higher MPS of 0.6, a smaller multiplier of \(1/0.6 \approx 1.67\), and a total impact of only \(80 \times 1.67 \approx \$133.3\) million, well below the \(\$200\) million in part (c). More of each round leaks into saving instead of being re-spent.
